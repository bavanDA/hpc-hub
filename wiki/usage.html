Usage and SUs

Your group's SUs, who used them, your storage, how busy the queues are, and what each queue allows.

What an SU is#

An SU (service unit) is the unit your cluster charges for work. Rates depend on the cluster, the queue and the resources. Your group (your lab's allocation) has a number of SUs, and jobs billed to it use them.

The tabs#

Pick the cluster at the top, then a tab.

Overview#

One card per group: SUs left, a bar of what is used with your part in it, and an estimate of how long the SUs last at the pace of past use. A red tag says Near the limit or Used up. Select a card to see who used it.

Hours#

For a group and a period: the hours used and Who used it. Select a person to see their jobs.

Storage#

Your quotas and how full each one is. Scratch rows say when files are deleted, with See what is at risk (Back up scratch).

Queues#

How busy each queue is now: busy CPUs, running jobs and waiting jobs. This doesn't promise a start time.

Limits#

The most one job can ask for on each queue you can use, read from Slurm. Learn about queues in the node window opens this too.

ColumnWhat it says
QueueThe name, whether it is the default, its nodes, and whole nodes only or other jobs can stop yours where they apply.
Longest timeThe longest a job may run, and the time you get if you don't ask.
Nodes, CoresThe most one job may use.
Memory per nodeThe memory limit, and memory per core where the queue sets one.
GPUs per nodeHow many and which kind, on clusters with GPUs.
Cost an hourIn SUs per core, GPU or node, or Free.

Priorities, under the table, lists each priority's longest time and caps. A job's priority can set a shorter time or a lower cap than its queue.

When numbers are missing#

Clusters keep different records. Where a cluster doesn't say, HPC Hub says so instead of guessing.
