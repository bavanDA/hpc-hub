HPC Hub

One app for the computing clusters you use for research. Sign in once, get a compute node, and keep your terminals, jobs, files and AI agents in one window, on your computer or your Android phone.

What it is#

A cluster (or HPC system, for high performance computing) is a large group of computers that a university shares among its researchers. Using one usually means a VPN, an SSH client, a Duo approval, a few Slurm commands to get a machine to work on, and a separate tool to move files. HPC Hub puts all of that behind one Connect button.

Once you are connected you can open a terminal, start VS Code or an AI coding agent on your own compute node, look at your jobs and your group's hours, browse and copy files, and back up scratch before the cluster deletes it. Terminals and agents keep running on the cluster when you close the laptop.

HPC Hub runs on Linux, Windows and macOS. There is also an Android app that does the same things on a phone.

Clusters it knows#

HPC Hub comes set up for three clusters:

  • Wulver at NJIT
  • Bouchet at Yale
  • Expanse at SDSC

On the first start you pick the ones you use. You can change that later in Settings.

New to HPC? A few words first#

These words come up all over the app and this wiki.

Login node
The shared computer you land on when you sign in. Fine for editing files and submitting jobs. Heavy work does not belong here, since everyone on the cluster shares it.
Compute node
One computer in the cluster that runs your work. You borrow it for a set time, with the cores and memory you asked for. HPC Hub can get one for you every time you connect.
Job
One request for cluster time. Your compute node, VS Code and agents each run as a job.
Slurm
The program most clusters use to hand out compute nodes and run jobs. HPC Hub talks to it for you.
Queue
Which group of nodes and time limits your job uses. Slurm calls it a partition.
Priority
How your job is billed and how soon it starts. Slurm calls it QoS.
Group
The account your hours are billed to, usually your lab or your advisor's allocation.
SU
Service units, the cluster's currency. 1 SU is roughly 1 core for 1 hour.
Scratch
Fast storage for working files. Most clusters delete old files there after a set number of days.
Session
A terminal or agent that keeps running on the cluster, even when the app is closed.

The pages in the app#

The sidebar on the left of the app (the menu at the top left on a phone) has these pages:

PageWhat it is for
ClustersConnect, get a compute node, open a terminal, VS Code, Files or an agent.
ProjectsNamed folders on your clusters, opened in one click, copied between clusters.
TerminalsTerminal sessions in tmux, with the timeline and marks.
CodeA light editor for files on the cluster. Text files you open from Files land here.
AgentsRunning agents, Backup of scratch, and what agents may run without asking.
PapersLaTeX papers. Experimental, hidden until you turn it on.
JobsYour jobs, your group's jobs and your history, with details and cancel.
UsageSUs left, storage, how busy the queues are, and queue limits.
FilesBrowse, preview, edit, upload and download, and copy between clusters, also with Globus.

Settings is at the bottom of the sidebar.
