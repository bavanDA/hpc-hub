HPC Hub

HPC Hub connects your computer to research computing clusters. Use it to get a compute node, open tools on it, manage files and check jobs.

Start here#

You need an account on a supported cluster before you can connect.

  1. Install HPC Hub on your computer.
  2. Follow First sign-in to choose your clusters and set up your login.
  3. Read Compute nodes and queues to choose resources for your work.
  4. Open a terminal or VS Code on your cluster card.

If setup fails, see Troubleshooting.

Supported clusters#

  • Wulver at NJIT
  • Bouchet at Yale
  • Expanse at SDSC

Choose the ones you use on the first start. You can show or hide them later in Settings. To add another cluster to HPC Hub, see this question.

HPC Hub runs on Windows, macOS and Linux. See Android app for the phone.

Four terms to know#

Cluster
A group of computers shared by researchers. HPC means high performance computing.
Login node
The shared computer you reach when you sign in. Use it to edit files and submit jobs. Run demanding work on a compute node.
Compute node
A computer that runs your work with the resources given to your job.
Job
A request for resources for a set time. Slurm, the cluster's scheduler, decides when it starts.

Work on a compute node stops when its job ends. Closing HPC Hub does not stop your jobs.

After you connect#

  • Jobs: check progress, see output paths and cancel jobs.
  • Files and transfers: browse, edit, upload, download and copy between clusters.
  • Projects: save folders and reopen them in your tools.
  • Back up scratch: find files that may be deleted and copy the ones you need.
  • Usage and SUs: check your group's allocation and storage.
  • AI agents: run Claude, ChatGPT, Antigravity or Cursor on a compute node.
