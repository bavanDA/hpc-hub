Phone app

The Android app has the same pages as the desktop, made for a phone. Sign it in by scanning a code from your computer.

The phone app is not on any store yet. See Install.

Scan from your computer#

The first start asks which clusters you use, then for each one offers Scan from your computer, Type it instead or Do this later.

To scan:

  1. On your computer, open Settings in HPC Hub and choose Set up the phone app. (The ⋮ menu on a cluster card has Connect phone for just that cluster.)
  2. Pick the logins to send. Include passwords: on, the phone signs in by itself; off, the phone asks for each password.
  3. Press Show QR code and scan it with the phone. The code hides itself after a minute.

One code can carry several clusters. For a cluster that uses SSH keys, the phone gets a key of its own instead of a copy of yours. Duo still asks when the phone connects.

Finding your way around#

The menu at the top left has the same pages, in the same order, as the desktop's sidebar, and Settings at the bottom. Android's Back closes the sheet on top first, then goes to Clusters. Long-press a row (a session, a file, a project) for its actions.

Stay connected in the background#

Android can close HPC Hub's connection when you lock the phone or switch apps. Two settings keep it open, under Settings, On the phone:

  • Stay connected in the background: keeps your connections open, with a notification like Connected to Wulver. It uses a little more battery while a cluster is connected.
  • Run without battery limits: so Android doesn't close your connections to save battery. Tap Turn on and Android asks you to allow it.

The app offers both once, after your first connect. If you said Not now, turn them on in Settings.

Notifications#

The phone can tell you when Duo is waiting, a cluster connected, your node's time is almost up, a job ended, a copy finished, an agent answered, scratch files are about to be deleted, or a backup finished. Pick which ones in Settings, Notifications. Tapping one opens the right page.

Terminals on the phone#

  • Swipe to scroll. Swipe up and down on the terminal to move through its history, as a mouse wheel would. A tap opens the keyboard.
  • The key row over the keyboard has Esc, Tab, Ctrl, Alt, the arrows and a few symbols that are hard to reach on a phone keyboard. Tap Ctrl or Alt to see the keys that go with it, like ^C.
  • The timeline works as on the desktop. The marks list opens from the bottom, and Back leaves the history. Marks you made on the computer are there too.

VPN on the phone#

The phone uses the same university VPN, but only for HPC Hub's own connections. Nothing else on the phone goes through it, and several clusters can be connected at once.

Your passwords on the phone#

The phone keeps logins in an encrypted file in the app's private storage. Its key is held by the phone's Android Keystore and never leaves the phone, and the file is left out of Android backups.
